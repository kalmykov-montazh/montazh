The quickest simple way from Barcelona Airport (El Prat) to the city centre is the **Aerobús**: about **€7.75** one way and roughly **35 minutes** to Plaça de Catalunya, straight from both terminals. The cheapest is the **R2 Nord train** from Terminal 2 (around **€4.60–4.90**, 19 minutes to Sants) or **bus 46** to Plaça d'Espanya. A **taxi** costs about **€30–45** to the centre, and a **private transfer** booked in advance starts at roughly €40–50 per car with a driver waiting at arrivals.

Below you will find every option compared, how to get from each terminal, what to do if you land at night, and the mistakes that cost travellers time and money. Prices are as of late 2026 and change every year or two, so treat them as a guide.

> Quick answer: light luggage and going to Plaça de Catalunya → Aerobús. Tight budget and landing at T2 → R2 Nord train. Family, heavy bags or late at night → taxi or a pre-booked transfer

## Terminal 1 or Terminal 2: check this first

El Prat has two terminals about 4 km apart, and the best route into town depends on where you land.

- **Terminal 1 (T1)** — most big airlines and alliances, plus long-haul flights
- **Terminal 2 (T2)** — many low-cost airlines (T2B and T2C)

A free shuttle bus links T1 and T2. It runs every few minutes and takes about 10–15 minutes. Your boarding pass or the airline's app tells you the terminal. If you land at T1 and want the train, factor in that shuttle ride.

## Aerobús: the easiest option for most people

The Aerobús is a dedicated airport express bus. Line A1 serves Terminal 1 and line A2 serves Terminal 2. Both go to **Plaça de Catalunya**, right in the centre, stopping on the way at Plaça d'Espanya and around Gran Via.

- **Price:** about **€7.75** one way and **€13.30** return (the return half is valid for 90 days). Some sites still show the older €7.45 / €12.85
- **Frequency:** every 5–10 minutes during the day, less often late at night
- **Time:** around 35 minutes to Plaça de Catalunya, up to 50 minutes at rush hour
- **Payment:** contactless bank card on board or at the machine at the stop, or the official app. Regular metro cards such as the T-casual are **not** valid

The Aerobús makes sense if your hotel is near Plaça de Catalunya, the Gothic Quarter, the Raval or the lower part of the Eixample. With big suitcases it is still comfortable: there is luggage space and no changes.

> Going back to the airport, check which line you need. The A1 goes to Terminal 1 and the A2 to Terminal 2 — getting on the wrong one means a shuttle ride on top

## Metro L9 Sud: good for some neighbourhoods, not for the centre

Metro line L9 Sud has stations at both terminals, but it **does not go directly into the centre**. You change at Zona Universitària (or at other interchanges along the line) to lines that reach the middle of town.

- **Price:** the special **airport ticket costs €5.90**. The standard T-casual 10-trip card is **not** valid for the airport stations. Travel passes such as the Hola Barcelona card, the T-dia or the T-usual do include the airport
- **Time:** 40–55 minutes to Plaça de Catalunya, including the change
- **Frequency:** roughly every 7 minutes

The metro works well if your accommodation is near an L9 station or along the line you change to (for example around Zona Universitària, Les Corts or the upper part of the city). For the Gothic Quarter or the beach, the Aerobús or the train is usually simpler.

## R2 Nord train: the cheapest fast option from Terminal 2

Rodalies (local trains) line **R2 Nord** leaves from the station at **Terminal 2**. From Terminal 1 you first take the free shuttle bus to T2.

- **Price:** about **€4.60–4.90** for a single ticket from the machine. Check at the machine whether your travel card covers the trip
- **Frequency:** every 30 minutes, from about 5:40 to about 23:30
- **Time:** about **19 minutes to Barcelona Sants** and 26 minutes to **Passeig de Gràcia**
- **Stations on the way:** Bellvitge, Barcelona Sants, Passeig de Gràcia, El Clot-Aragó

If you are catching a high-speed train onwards (to Madrid, Valencia, Seville or France), the R2 Nord to Sants is the logical choice — Sants is Barcelona's main station. ## Bus 46 and the night buses

**Bus 46** is a regular city bus from both terminals to **Plaça d'Espanya**. It is the cheapest option (about €2.55–2.90), but slow: 45–60 minutes, every 15–20 minutes, from about 5:30 until around midnight. It is fine with a backpack, less fun with two suitcases.

At night, when the train and most metro trains have stopped, there are **night buses** to Plaça de Catalunya:

- **N17** — from Terminal 1
- **N18** — from both terminals
- **N16** — from Terminal 2 via Plaça d'Espanya (listed by some sources)

They run roughly every 20 minutes, from late evening until early morning, and take 40–60 minutes. You pay the standard bus fare.

## Taxi: price, supplements and how not to overpay

Official Barcelona taxis are black and yellow and wait at the marked ranks outside both terminals.

- **To the centre:** usually **€30–45** on the meter, depending on traffic, time of day and the exact address
- **Airport supplement:** about **€4.50–4.60** is added to the meter
- **Night and weekend rates** are higher than daytime weekday rates
- **Cruise port:** there is a fixed fare of about **€46** from the airport to the cruise terminals (Moll Adossat)
- **Time:** 20–35 minutes

Rules that save money and nerves:

1. Take a taxi only from the official rank, not from someone who approaches you inside the terminal
2. Make sure the meter is switched on when you leave
3. Card payment is standard in Barcelona taxis — say before you go if you want to pay by card
4. Ride-hailing apps (Uber, Cabify) pick up from their own zones, not from the taxi rank — follow the signs in the app

## Private transfer: when it is worth it

A pre-booked transfer means a driver waits at arrivals with your name on a sign and the price is fixed when you book — no meter, no surprises with traffic or night rates.

It is worth it when:

- you are a family or group of 3–4 people (per person it can cost about the same as the Aerobús)
- you land late at night or very early in the morning
- you have children, a pushchair, a lot of luggage or reduced mobility
- you are going outside Barcelona — Sitges, Salou, Girona or the Costa Brava

Prices in Barcelona usually start at roughly €40–50 per car to the centre. You can compare fixed-price transfers on [Kiwitaxi](https://kiwitaxi.tpk.ro/11m18X2U): the price includes waiting time and flight tracking, so if your flight is late the driver still meets you.

## Renting a car at Barcelona Airport

For the city itself a car is more of a burden than a help: parking is expensive, and Barcelona has a low-emission zone (ZBE) with restrictions for older cars. A car makes sense if you are heading straight out — to the Costa Brava, Montserrat, the Pyrenees or along the coast to Valencia.

All the big rental companies are at the airport. ## Flight delayed or cancelled?

If your flight to or from Barcelona arrived more than 3 hours late or was cancelled at short notice, EU rules (Regulation 261/2004) may entitle you to compensation of €250–600 per passenger, depending on the distance — unless the airline can prove extraordinary circumstances. How it works is explained in [Flight delay compensation in Spain](/en/guides/flight-delay-compensation-spain). ## Common mistakes

- **Buying a T-casual for the airport metro.** It doesn't work at the airport stations — you need the €5.90 airport ticket or a pass that includes the airport
- **Waiting for the train at Terminal 1.** The R2 Nord leaves only from Terminal 2
- **Taking the metro to the Gothic Quarter with big bags.** L9 needs a change and long walks between platforms; the Aerobús is simpler
- **Getting on the wrong Aerobús on the way back.** A1 is for T1, A2 for T2
- **Accepting a ride from someone inside the terminal.** Use the official taxi rank or a pre-booked transfer
- **Forgetting the night.** After about 23:30 the train stops; plan a night bus, taxi or transfer

## Which option should you choose?

- **Solo or a couple, light luggage, staying in the centre:** Aerobús
- **Landing at T2 on a budget, or catching a train at Sants:** R2 Nord
- **Staying near an L9 station or with a travel pass:** metro
- **Family, group, a lot of luggage or late arrival:** taxi or private transfer
- **Going straight to the coast or the mountains:** rental car

## FAQ

### How much is the Aerobús from Barcelona Airport?

About €7.75 one way and €13.30 return as of late 2026, with the return half valid for 90 days. You can pay by contactless card on board or at the stop.

### Is there a train from Barcelona Airport Terminal 1?

No. The R2 Nord train leaves only from Terminal 2. From Terminal 1, take the free shuttle bus to T2 first (about 10–15 minutes).

### Can I use the T-casual card to get to the airport?

Not on the metro: the airport stations need the special €5.90 airport ticket. Passes such as Hola Barcelona, T-dia and T-usual do include the airport.

### How much is a taxi from Barcelona Airport to the city centre?

Usually €30–45 on the meter, plus an airport supplement of about €4.50–4.60. Night and weekend rates are higher. Trips to the cruise port have a fixed fare of about €46.

### What is the fastest way from Barcelona Airport to the city?

A taxi or private transfer (20–35 minutes) when traffic is light. By public transport, the R2 Nord train from T2 takes about 19 minutes to Sants, and the Aerobús about 35 minutes to Plaça de Catalunya.

### How do I get from Barcelona Airport to the city at night?

Night buses N17 (from T1) and N18 (from both terminals) go to Plaça de Catalunya roughly every 20 minutes. Otherwise a taxi or a transfer booked in advance.

### How long does it take to get from Barcelona Airport to Sants station?

About 19 minutes on the R2 Nord train from Terminal 2, or 20–35 minutes by taxi depending on traffic.
