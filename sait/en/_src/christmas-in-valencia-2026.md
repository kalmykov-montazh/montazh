Christmas in Valencia runs longer than in the UK or the US: it starts in early December and only ends on 6 January, the day of the Three Kings, which is when Spanish children open most of their presents. Expect lights in the old town, a huge nativity scene inside City Hall, markets near the Central Market and a big evening parade on 5 January.

## When the lights go on

The main lights and the giant tree are in Plaza del Ayuntamiento, the City Hall square. In 2025 they were switched on on 21 November.

This year the date is later. As of late November 2026 the City Council had not confirmed it, and local press were reporting that the switch-on was being planned for **early December** because the square had been occupied until mid-November. If you are coming for the opening night, check the Visit València agenda the week before.

Once they are on, the lights usually stay up until 6 January.

## The nativity scene at City Hall

Every year a large nativity scene (belén) is set up in the Salón de Cristal inside City Hall. It opens on **1 December**; local sources give the closing date as 3 or 6 January, so check before you go. Entry is free and the queue moves quickly on weekday mornings.

Spanish nativity scenes are not just a stable and three figures. They are whole miniature towns with markets, rivers and tiny people at work, and families go round them hunting for details.

## Christmas markets

Valencia's markets are smaller than German ones, more crafts and gifts than mulled wine. The ones to know:

- **Central Market area** — the Christmas and Kings market is announced for 10 December to 4 January, open roughly from 9:00 to 22:00
- **City of Arts and Sciences** — a market among the futuristic buildings, usually from mid-December to early January
- **Plaza de la Reina and around the Cathedral** — craft stalls and nativity figures
- **Cabanyal** — a small Kings market by the sea just before 6 January

Exact dates for some of them are still being published in late November 2026, so treat them as a guide.

## Expojove for families

Expojove, now called La Central de Nadal, is a huge indoor fair for children at Feria València, with games, sports and workshops. In 2026 it runs from **26 December to 4 January**. It is a good plan for a rainy day or a family with younger kids. Feria València is outside the centre, so check the metro or book a taxi.

## The Three Kings parade on 5 January

The Cabalgata de Reyes is the biggest Christmas night in Valencia. The Kings arrive by sea in the afternoon, then the parade starts around **17:30** and goes through the centre to Plaza del Ayuntamiento, with floats and sweets thrown into the crowd.

> Come at least 40 minutes early and stand on the route rather than in the square, where it gets packed. Bring a bag for the sweets: children here take this part very seriously

## Bank holidays to plan around

- 8 December — Immaculate Conception
- 25 December — Christmas Day
- 1 January — New Year's Day
- 6 January — Epiphany, Three Kings Day

On these days many shops are shut, and buses and the metro run reduced timetables. Shops do open on several Sundays in December for Christmas shopping.

## Where to stay

For Christmas the old town (Ciutat Vella) puts you next to the lights, the nativity scene and the parade route, but it is the most expensive. Ruzafa is a 10-minute walk from City Hall, full of bars and cheaper. If you want the beach, Cabanyal and the Malvarrosa seafront are quieter and well connected by tram.

Prices rise sharply for the 5 January parade weekend and for New Year's Eve, so book those nights first and choose free cancellation in case plans change.

## Getting around

From Valencia Airport the metro takes about 25 minutes to the centre. If you land late or with children and luggage, a pre-booked transfer saves a lot of hassle on busy holiday evenings. A Spanish or European eSIM bought before you fly means maps and tickets work from the moment you land.

Valencia at Christmas is calm in the mornings and buzzing in the evenings, so leave space in your plans for a slow walk under the lights with a hot chocolate and churros
