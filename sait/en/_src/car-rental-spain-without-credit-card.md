It is one of the most common scenes at Spanish airports: the car is booked and paid for, but at the desk you hear "we need a credit card in the main driver's name". Debit cards are refused and the booking is lost. You can avoid this before you pay.

## Why rental companies want a credit card

The company blocks a deposit on your card in case of damage or fines. That is easier and cheaper for them with a credit card, so most companies write "credit card only" in their terms. The deposit varies a lot: €200–300 at some companies, up to €1,000–1,500 at others.

> You can almost always pay for the booking online with a debit card. That payment goes to the booking website. At the rental desk you need a separate card for the deposit — and that is where they check whether it is a credit card

## When you can get a car with a debit card

Some companies accept a Visa or Mastercard debit card for the deposit, usually on condition that you buy their full insurance at the desk. The deposit is then lower or zero, but the daily price goes up.

What to check before you pay:

1. In the booking conditions, find the section on payment and deposit
2. Look for "debit card accepted"
3. Check the deposit amount and what changes if you take the company's insurance
4. The card must be in the main driver's name — not a partner's or a friend's

## Where to find these offers

The easiest way is to compare all rental companies in one search and read each company's conditions. On DiscoverCars you can see for every offer whether a debit card is accepted.

## A few more things to check

- Licence: the rental terms say which licences are accepted and whether you need an International Driving Permit — check before paying, [rules for UK and US licences](/en/guides/driving-in-spain-uk-us-licence)
- Age: many companies charge extra for drivers under 25
- Fuel: "full to full" is usually the cheapest option
- Take photos of the car from every side when you pick it up — two minutes now saves an argument later
