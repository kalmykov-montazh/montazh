The City of Arts and Sciences is the white, futuristic complex at the end of Valencia's Turia park, and the Oceanogràfic, Europe's largest aquarium, sits inside it. Walking around the buildings is free. You only pay to go inside: the Oceanogràfic on its own costs from about €37 for an adult, and the ticket for all three main venues is about €45.55 (official prices, November 2026).

## What is actually there

The complex stretches for almost two kilometres along the old riverbed, with pools and gardens between the buildings. Three of them need a ticket:

- **Oceanogràfic** — the aquarium, with sharks, belugas, penguins, a dolphinarium and a long underwater tunnel. This is the one families come for
- **Museu de les Ciències** — the science museum, hands-on and good for kids. You can walk into the building for free, the exhibitions need a ticket
- **Hemisfèric** — the eye-shaped building with a giant dome cinema. One ticket covers one film of about 45 minutes, and latecomers are not let in

The rest you can see without paying: the Umbracle, an open-air garden walk of more than 17,000 m² with palms and sculptures, the outside of the Palau de les Arts opera house and the Àgora.

## Ticket options and prices

Official prices from Valencia's tourist office shop, November 2026:

- **All three venues** (Oceanogràfic, Science Museum, Hemisfèric): adult about €45.55, child 4–12 and over-65 about €35.80
- **Oceanogràfic + Science Museum**: adult about €38.90, reduced about €29.40
- **Oceanogràfic only**: from about €37 for an adult, up to around €43 on busy dates (prices vary by day)
- **Science Museum + Hemisfèric**: from about €14

Children under 3 go in free everywhere.

> The three-venue ticket does not have to be used in one go. You can split it over two or three consecutive days, which is much kinder on small children

Tickets cannot be refunded, but the date can usually be changed with at least 48 hours' notice. On holidays and in summer, buy online a few days ahead: the queue at the Oceanogràfic box office can easily eat half an hour.

## How long to plan

- Oceanogràfic: 3–4 hours if you want to see the shows and not rush
- Science Museum: 2–3 hours
- Hemisfèric: about an hour including getting seated
- all three in one day: 6–7 hours, so start at opening time

Opening times change with the season. Roughly, everything opens at 10:00 and closes between 18:00 in winter and 20:00–21:00 in summer. Last entry is an hour before closing, so check the exact hours for your date on the official site before you go.

## Getting there

The complex is at Av. del Professor López Piñero 7, at the south-eastern end of the Turia gardens.

- **On foot or by bike** — the nicest way. Walk or cycle down the Turia park from the old town; it is flat, green and has no traffic
- **By bus** — several EMT lines stop next to it, including 19, 40 and 95
- **By taxi** — a short ride from the centre, handy with kids at the end of the day

> If you arrive at Valencia airport and head straight here with luggage, book a transfer to your hotel first. Most hotels hold bags, and the Oceanogràfic is no fun with a suitcase

## Where to stay nearby

Staying close to the complex makes sense for families: you can go back to the hotel for a nap between venues. The area around the City of Arts and Sciences has modern hotels and apartments, and the beach is a short bus or taxi ride away. Compare a few booking sites before you pay, prices for the same room can differ.

## Worth combining with

The Oceanogràfic is a morning. For the rest of the day, the Turia park leads straight back to the old town, and in the afternoon you can get out to the [Albufera lagoon](/en/guides/albufera-boat-trip-and-paella-from-valencia) for a boat ride and a paella. A half-day tour does both without having to work out buses.

Mornings on weekdays are the quietest time to see the sharks without a crowd at the glass
