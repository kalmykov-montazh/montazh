The high-speed line between Madrid and Andalusia is still closed, and the low-cost operator **Ouigo** has now cancelled all its trains on the route until **at least Thursday 3 December**. The line has been shut since the serious accident near Urda (Toledo) on **28 October**. A judge allowed the rail infrastructure company Adif to start rebuilding the damaged track on **19 November**, but Adif says it still has no official date for when the works will finish. If you planned to take the train from Madrid to Córdoba, Seville or Málaga in the next couple of weeks, this affects you.

Ouigo first cancelled its trains until 22 November, then until 29 November, and now until 3 December. It has said itself that 3 December is not a confirmed restart date. According to Telemadrid, no operator — Renfe, Iryo or Ouigo — is running high-speed trains on the line right now.

## What it means for you

- **Have a Ouigo ticket?** You get a full refund. Ouigo emails affected passengers, so check your inbox and spam folder.
- **Bus instead of the train:** Ouigo refunds bus tickets bought on Movelia for a cancelled Ouigo journey. This applies until 3 December.
- **Tickets for mid-December:** Renfe, Iryo and Ouigo have reopened sales between Seville and Madrid from **13 December**, but Adif has not confirmed the line will be ready by then. If you book, pick a fare you can change or cancel.
- **Other ways to go:** domestic flights from Madrid to Seville and Málaga take about an hour and a car takes around five hours to Seville. With a group of three or four, a hire car often costs less than last-minute flights.
- **Hotel booked in Andalusia?** Choose free cancellation for now, so a change of dates costs nothing.

Check the operator's website the day before you travel: the dates have moved three times in a month
