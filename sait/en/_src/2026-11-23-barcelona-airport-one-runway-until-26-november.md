Barcelona-El Prat, Spain's second busiest airport, is running on just one runway until **Thursday 26 November 2026**. Aena closed two of its three runways on 16 November for works, and around **1,441 take-off and landing slots**, roughly 15% of normal capacity, have been taken out of the schedule. If you are flying to or from Barcelona this week, this is worth a quick check before you leave for the airport.

The works are on the cross runway 02/20, where Aena is building a new rapid exit so planes can leave the runway faster. Runway 06L/24R is also closed during this phase. The project costs about €3.1 million and, according to Aena, should mean less waiting in the air and on the ground once it is finished. While the works run, the airport handles about 22 arrivals and 22 departures an hour.

## What it means for you

Airlines were told about the cut in advance and most have already built it into their timetables. So the risk is not a surprise cancellation at the gate but a flight that was quietly moved or merged with another one weeks ago. Open your airline's app or website and check the current departure time, not the one in your original confirmation email.

Make sure the airline has your mobile number and email on the booking, so any change reaches you. If you have a connection, check that the gap is still long enough. If it has become too tight, ask the airline or your agent to move you to another flight now rather than at the airport.

Leave a little extra time on the way to El Prat, especially at peak hours, as queues for take-off can mean delays even on flights that are running.

Under EU261 and UK261, if your flight is cancelled you can choose between a refund and an alternative flight. Fixed compensation of up to €600 usually does not apply when the airline told you about the cancellation at least 14 days in advance. A long delay on the day is a different matter and can still qualify, so keep your boarding pass and any messages from the airline.

From 27 November the single-runway phase ends. Some night closures will continue into the first half of December, but Aena says they will not affect normal operations
