The best eSIM for Spain is a data plan you buy online before you fly, install with a QR code at home and switch on when you land. For a one-week holiday a small plan of a few gigabytes is enough and usually costs **a few dollars or euros**; for a longer stay or a trip across several countries, a Europe-wide plan saves you buying a new one at every border. Two well-known options are [Yesim](https://yesim.tpk.ro/P3EoaA79) and [Airalo](https://airalo.tpk.ro/9SV4RThp).

An eSIM is a SIM card without the plastic. Your data works as soon as you land — no roaming bill and no hunting for a phone shop at the airport. Below: how to check your phone, how to install it, what roaming from the UK or the US actually costs, and when a Spanish SIM makes more sense.

## Do you even need an eSIM

It depends on where your phone contract is from:

- **EU SIM** (France, Germany, Ireland and the rest of the EU): you probably don't need one. EU "roam like at home" rules let you use your normal allowance in Spain at no extra charge, with a fair-use limit
- **UK SIM**: since Brexit, free EU roaming is a plan feature, not a law. Some plans still include it, many newer ones charge per day
- **US, Canada, Latin America, Asia**: roaming is usually expensive — an eSIM is almost always cheaper

## What roaming costs from the US (2026)

The big US carriers sell day passes: AT&T International Day Pass and Verizon TravelPass are about **$12 a day** per line, T-Mobile has a 1-day pass at about **$10** plus longer passes. For a week, that is $70–85 per phone.

Without a pass, pay-per-use data abroad can cost dollars per megabyte. Before you fly, either buy a pass or switch off data roaming and use an eSIM.

## Does your phone support eSIM

Most iPhone, Samsung Galaxy and Google Pixel models from the last few years do. To check:

- iPhone: Settings → Mobile Data (Cellular) → you should see "Add eSIM"
- Android: Settings → Connections → SIM manager → "Add eSIM"

> Your phone must be unlocked. If you got it on contract from a carrier, check this before you travel

## When to buy and how to install

1. Buy the data plan at home, before your trip
2. Install it on Wi-Fi by scanning the QR code from the email — it takes about a minute
3. Leave it switched off until you land in Spain: the plan usually starts counting from the first connection
4. On arrival, turn on the eSIM line for mobile data and data roaming on that line
5. Keep your main SIM on for calls and bank text messages, but turn off data roaming on it

## How much data you need

Compare plans by gigabytes and validity:

- **one week**, maps, messaging, a few photos — 3–5 GB
- **two weeks** with social media and video calls — 10 GB or more
- **a month**, working remotely or streaming — a big plan or unlimited

If you are also visiting Portugal, France or Italy, take a Europe plan. Crossing a border with a Spain-only plan means buying a second one.

## eSIM or a Spanish SIM card

- **holiday or a few weeks** — an eSIM is simpler: everything is ready before you leave, no paperwork, no shops
- **moving to Spain** — you will need a local SIM with a Spanish number. Banks, government offices and appointment systems send their codes by text message to a Spanish number

Most travel eSIMs give you data only, without a Spanish phone number. For WhatsApp and calls over the internet that is fine.

## Common mistakes

- buying the eSIM at the airport on arrival without Wi-Fi to install it
- deleting the eSIM after the trip while there is still data left — with most providers a deleted eSIM can't be reinstalled
- leaving data roaming on for the main SIM: the phone may quietly use the expensive line
- buying a Spain-only plan for a trip that also includes Portugal or France

## FAQ

### Do I need an eSIM for Spain?

If your SIM is from an EU country, usually not: roaming is included. With a UK, US or other non-EU SIM, an eSIM is usually much cheaper than roaming.

### Is roaming free in Spain for UK phones?

Only on some plans. O2 includes EU roaming on most monthly plans; EE, Vodafone and Three include it on some plans and charge around £2–2.75 a day on others.

### When should I install my eSIM?

At home, on Wi-Fi, a day or two before you fly. Switch it on when you land, because most plans start counting from the first connection.

### How much data do I need for a week in Spain?

For maps, messaging and some social media, 3–5 GB is usually enough. Take more if you make video calls or work online.

### Will I get a Spanish phone number with an eSIM?

Most travel eSIMs are data-only. If you need a Spanish number for banks or government appointments, get a local SIM after you arrive.

### Does a Spain eSIM work in Portugal or France?

A Spain-only plan does not. For several countries, buy a Europe plan
