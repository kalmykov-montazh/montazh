British, American, Canadian and Australian citizens can visit Spain without a visa for short stays. The key limit is the 90/180 rule — and since 2026 it is checked electronically.

## How the 90/180 rule works

You can spend up to 90 days in any 180-day period in the Schengen Area — Spain plus 28 other European countries. It is not "90 days per trip" and it does not reset when you leave.

To check if you can travel on a given date: look back 180 days from that date and add up every day you spent in any Schengen country. Days of entry and exit both count. If the total, including your planned trip, is over 90, you need to shorten the trip or wait.

> The EU has an official short-stay calculator: search for "Schengen short-stay calculator" on the European Commission website

## What EES changes

Since 10 April 2026 the EU Entry/Exit System (EES) is fully operational at Schengen borders. Instead of passport stamps, your entries and exits are recorded digitally, with your fingerprints and a facial photo on your first crossing. You do not need to do anything before you travel, but your first entry may take a little longer. Overstays are now detected automatically, so counting your days matters more than before.

## And ETIAS

ETIAS is a future online travel authorisation for visa-free visitors, similar to the US ESTA. It is not running yet and no applications are being taken; the EU has said it will announce the start date several months in advance. Be careful with websites that offer an "ETIAS application" today

## If you want to stay longer than 90 days

Longer stays need a national visa or residence permit, such as Spain's non-lucrative visa or the digital nomad visa. These are applied for in advance and have their own requirements, so plan months ahead
