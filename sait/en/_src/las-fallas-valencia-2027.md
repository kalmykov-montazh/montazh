Las Fallas is Valencia's biggest festival and one of the most spectacular in Europe. Every year in March the city fills with giant sculptures, fireworks and parades, and on the last night almost all of them are burned.

## When is Fallas 2027

The main days are **15 to 19 March 2027**, with events throughout the first weeks of March.

- **1–19 March**: the mascletà every day at 2 pm in Plaza del Ayuntamiento — a deafening daytime firecracker show
- **15–16 March**: the "plantà" — the fallas (sculptures) are set up all over the city
- **17–18 March**: the flower offering to the Virgin; a huge flower tapestry grows in Plaza de la Virgen
- **19 March**: the Cremà — the fallas are burned at night, the last one in Plaza del Ayuntamiento

## Where to stay

Hotels fill up and prices rise sharply for Fallas week, so book as early as you can. Staying in the centre puts you close to everything but it is noisy day and night. Areas a short metro ride away (Benimaclet, Campanar, near the beach in Cabanyal) are calmer and often cheaper.

## Getting around

Many central streets are closed to traffic during the festival. The metro and walking are the easiest ways around; a car is more trouble than it is worth in the centre. If you arrive late at night, a pre-booked airport transfer saves queuing for a taxi.

## Practical tips

- arrive at the mascletà square at least an hour early
- bring earplugs, especially for children
- keep an eye on your phone and wallet in the crowds
- the night of 19 March is the most crowded; for a calmer view of the burning, choose a smaller falla in a neighbourhood
