Spain holiday rentals: 10% VAT from 1 December 2026 — will your booking cost more?

From Tuesday 1 December 2026, furnished holiday flats and apartments in Spain rented to the same guest for 30 nights or fewer will carry 10% VAT. Until now most of them paid no VAT at all, as long as the owner didn't offer hotel-style services such as cleaning during the stay or fresh linen. The change comes from Royal Decree-law 29/2026, published in the official gazette (BOE) on 7 October and confirmed in Congress on 12 November. If you've booked a villa or an apartment for Christmas, New Year or a winter break, this may touch your bill.

The rule is linked to the date of the stay, not the date you booked. So a flat you reserved back in the summer for 20 December can still fall under the new VAT. Whether you actually pay more depends on the owner: some will add the 10% on top, others will keep the price and take the hit themselves.

Stays of more than 30 nights without hotel services stay VAT-free. Hotels, and rentals that already offered hotel-type services, were already at 10%, so nothing changes for them.

What it means for you

  • Already booked? Look at your confirmation. If it says the price includes taxes, the owner can't simply raise it afterwards. If you're unsure, message the host or the platform and ask whether VAT is included.
  • Booking now: compare the final price at checkout, not the nightly rate. On a €120-a-night flat for 4 nights, adding 10% VAT turns €480 into €528.
  • Tourist tax is separate. In Catalonia, the Balearics, the Basque Country and some cities it's charged on top, whatever the VAT.
  • Long stays: a booking of 31 nights or more is still VAT-free, unless the flat comes with hotel services.
  • Comparing with a hotel: with both now at 10%, the gap between a hotel and a holiday flat may be smaller than you think, especially for short city breaks
