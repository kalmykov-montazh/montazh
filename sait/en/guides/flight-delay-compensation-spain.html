Flight delayed or cancelled in Spain: how to claim up to €600 compensation

If your flight from or to Spain arrives three hours late or more, is cancelled at short notice, or you are denied boarding because of overbooking, you may be owed a fixed cash payment. It does not depend on the ticket price.

Which flights are covered

EU Regulation 261/2004 covers:

  • every flight departing from an airport in the EU (including Spain), whatever the airline
  • flights arriving in the EU on an EU airline

Flights to and from the UK are covered by the UK version of the same rules (UK261).

How much you can get

  • flights up to 1,500 km — €250 (£220 under UK261)
  • flights between 1,500 and 3,500 km, and intra-EU flights over 1,500 km — €400 (£350)
  • flights over 3,500 km — €600 (£520)

What matters is the delay at arrival: from three hours, counted to the moment the aircraft doors open.

When the airline does not have to pay

The airline can refuse if the delay was caused by extraordinary circumstances: severe weather, air traffic control restrictions, security threats, political unrest. Technical faults and crew shortages are generally not extraordinary — in those cases you are usually owed compensation.

Strikes by the airline's own staff usually do not free the airline from paying. Strikes by air traffic controllers or airport staff usually do

What to keep

  • your boarding pass or booking confirmation
  • a screenshot of the delay or cancellation message
  • receipts for food, hotel or taxi if you had to pay while waiting

During a long delay the airline must also give you meals and drinks, and a hotel if you have to stay overnight — whether or not cash compensation is due.

How to claim

You can write to the airline yourself through the claim form on its website. If they refuse or do not answer, a claim service takes over the case for a commission and only gets paid if you win. You can usually claim for flights from the past few years, so check old trips too
