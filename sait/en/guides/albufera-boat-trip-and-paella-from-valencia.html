Albufera boat trip and paella from Valencia: bus, tours, prices and the best time to go

L'Albufera is a huge freshwater lagoon surrounded by rice fields, about 10–15 km south of Valencia. Most visitors do three things there: a short ride on a traditional flat boat, a paella lunch in the village of El Palmar and, if they can, sunset over the water. You can do it on your own by city bus for a few euros, or book a tour that bundles transport, boat and lunch.

Why it is worth half a day

The lagoon is the largest in Spain and the heart of a natural park with hundreds of bird species. It is also rice country: the rice for Valencian paella is grown right here, and the area is widely seen as the home of the dish. El Palmar, a small village of canals and low houses, has around a dozen restaurants that cook paella over wood fire, plus local specials like all i pebre, eel stew with garlic and paprika.

Getting there by public bus

Valencia's city buses EMT 24 and 25 go to the park.

  • line 24 goes from the centre to El Palmar, the village with the restaurants
  • line 25 runs along the coast towards El Perellonet and stops near the main boat jetties
  • the ride takes under an hour
  • a single ticket costs about €1.50 (November 2026) and the Valencia Tourist Card covers it

Buses are less frequent than inside the city, especially at weekends, so check the return times in the EMT app before you go.

Plan the bus back before you sit down to lunch. Paella is cooked to order and takes 20–30 minutes, and the last bus after sunset is the one everyone wants

The boat ride

Boats are flat wooden albuferencs, pushed or motored slowly across the lagoon. A ride usually lasts 30–45 minutes.

The busiest departure point is the Gola de Pujol jetty on the coast road. You will also find boats in El Palmar itself and in smaller places like El Saler or Catarroja, where it is quieter. In El Palmar boatmen wait at the canals from mid-morning, and the price is agreed on the spot, so ask before you get in.

Sunset is the most popular slot: the lagoon turns red and gold. For a sunset ride, book ahead or arrive early.

Paella in El Palmar

Restaurants usually serve paella for a minimum of two people, and many only cook it at lunchtime. Expect roughly €12–18 per person with a drink at simpler places (November 2026), more at the well-known ones.

What to order:

  • paella valenciana — chicken, rabbit, green beans and garrofó beans, no seafood
  • arroz a banda — rice cooked in fish stock
  • all i pebre — eel stew, the local speciality

From March to October book a table for weekends. In winter some restaurants have shorter hours.

Tours: bus, boat and paella in one

If you do not want to deal with buses, a tour does everything for you. Typical options:

  • bus + boat — about 2 hours with an audio guide, no lunch
  • bus + boat + paella — 4–5 hours, with the boat ride and a set lunch in El Palmar
  • sunset boat tours and bike tours through the rice fields

Combined tours with transport, boat and lunch usually cost roughly €25–45 per person (November 2026). Check the cancellation terms before booking: some tours allow free cancellation up to 24 hours before, others are non-refundable.

By car or bike

By car it is about 20–25 minutes to El Palmar along the CV-500 coast road, which is the easiest option if you want to combine the lagoon with the beaches of El Saler and La Devesa, a strip of pine forest and dunes between the lagoon and the sea. Cyclists can follow the bike lanes south from the city, roughly 1.5 hours each way.

When to go

  • spring (April–June) — fields are flooded and freshly planted, mild weather, the best photos
  • September–October — harvest time, fewer crowds than summer, beautiful sunsets
  • summer — hot and busy at sunset, bring mosquito repellent
  • winter — quiet, lots of water birds, but check restaurant hours

Weekdays are always calmer than weekends. Go after lunch, take the boat before sunset and you get the best of the day in one trip
