ETIAS won't start in 2026: what UK and US travellers to Spain need for 2027 trips

With a month of 2026 left, ETIAS is still not running and the EU has not published a start date. For a long time the official target was the last quarter of 2026. That window has since disappeared from the EU's travel site, and as of mid-November no applications are being taken. Reports in the Financial Times back in July already said the launch would slip to 2027; the European Commission's line is only that the date will be set once everything is ready. If you're British or American and planning Spain for Easter, summer or a winter break next year, here's where things stand.

Nothing changes at the border for now. You still travel to Spain on your passport alone, within the 90/180 rule. The new Entry/Exit System (EES), which has been fully running since April 2026, is what registers your fingerprints and photo on arrival. ETIAS is a separate, later step.

When ETIAS does arrive, the rules already agreed are these: the fee is €20, under-18s and over-70s don't pay (but still need the authorisation), and it lasts up to 3 years or until your passport expires. For the first six months it will be optional, then there's a further grace period of about six months, so the strict check only bites around a year after launch.

What it means for you

  • Booking for 2027: you don't need anything beyond a valid passport today. Make sure it's less than 10 years old on the day you enter and valid for 3 months after you plan to leave.
  • Don't pay anyone for ETIAS now. Since the system isn't open, any site taking money for an "ETIAS application" is not official. The real one will be on europa.eu.
  • Watch for the announcement. The EU has promised several months' notice, and with the optional period on top, nobody will be turned away at short notice.
  • Counting your days: EES now does it automatically, so overstaying is easier to spot. Our guide to the 90/180 rule, EES and ETIAS explains how to count
