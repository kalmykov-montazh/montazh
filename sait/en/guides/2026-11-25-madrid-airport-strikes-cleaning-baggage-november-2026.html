Madrid airport strikes this week: cleaners and baggage system staff walk out

If you are flying through Madrid-Barajas in the next few days, two separate strikes overlap at the airport. Cleaning staff in Terminals 1, 2 and 3 start an open-ended strike at 23:00 on Wednesday 25 November, and the maintenance team for the automated baggage system has stoppages planned on 26, 27, 28, 29 and 30 November. Neither strike cancels flights by itself, but both can make the airport slower and less pleasant than usual.

The cleaners' strike involves around 280 workers employed by Serveo Facility Management. Talks with the company broke down overnight from 24 to 25 November, and the unions say the strike has no end date. Minimum services apply, so how visible the walkout will be is still unclear. Terminal 4, used by Iberia and most long-haul flights, is not part of this strike.

The baggage system strike is by maintenance staff at Outsmart Assistance. They stop work twice a day, 05:00–07:00 and 13:00–15:00, on Mondays, Thursdays, Fridays, Saturdays and Sundays, until 30 November. The Spanish government has set minimum services at 50% of scheduled staff. The risk is that if the conveyor system breaks down during those hours, repairs take longer and bags arrive late, including transfer bags on connecting flights. This one covers all terminals, T4 included.

What it means for you

  • Your flight should operate as normal. Check its status with your airline on the day rather than relying on airport rumours.
  • On 26–30 November, if your flight lands or departs around 05:00–07:00 or 13:00–15:00, put essentials, medicines and a change of clothes in your hand luggage.
  • On a tight connection through Madrid, a longer layover or hand luggage only lowers the chance of your bag being left behind.
  • If your bag does not show up, report it at the baggage desk before leaving the hall and keep the reference number. Under the Montreal Convention you can claim reasonable costs for essentials while you wait.
  • If your flight is cancelled or delayed by more than 3 hours, check whether you are owed compensation under EU261 or UK261. Strikes by airport contractors are often treated differently from airline staff strikes, so it is worth checking each case
